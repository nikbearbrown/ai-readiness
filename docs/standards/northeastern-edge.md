# Northeastern University: AI readiness definition and Edge's five standards

An institutional profile (see `README.md` in this folder). The framework the toolkit works from is `../framework.md`; this file says what Northeastern's Edge checks before it certifies a course as AI ready, so courses at Northeastern can be audited against it too. Paraphrased from Edge's faculty orientation material (2026). This toolkit is not an Edge tool and does not certify anything. Edge's learning experience designer (LXD) certifies; the toolkit prepares the course and the conversation.

## The four dimensions (university-wide)

Every program at Northeastern is expected to make its students AI ready along these four dimensions. They are deliberately broad so they fit any course at any level.

| # | Dimension | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Understanding AI and data | How AI works, its evolving impact, the role of data, how bias in data shapes output |
| D2 | Critical thinking and judgment | Evaluating AI output for accuracy, bias and relevance |
| D3 | Ethical and responsible use | Fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy; the system's conduct, not only the individual's |
| D4 | Human creativity and innovation | Humans stay at the core; AI is a thought partner, never a replacement |

## The five minimum standards (Edge course certification)

A course is certifiable only when all five hold. Read each one literally: the word that matters is in bold.

| # | Standard | The test the toolkit applies |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | The syllabus has an **explicit** AI use policy covering **both** how the instructor used AI to prepare the course **and** how students may use AI in it | Two halves, both in the syllabus text. A link to a policy elsewhere is partial: the certifier reads the syllabus. The instructor-preparation half can only come from the instructor; the toolkit never invents it |
| E2 | At least one **module-level** learning objective **explicitly** addresses one of D1–D4 | A course-level outcome alone is partial. "Use ChatGPT" is a tool, not a dimension; the objective must name AI and the thinking (evaluate, explain, decide, disclose, create) |
| E3 | At least one **designated** activity **requires** students to engage with AI on purpose as part of learning | Optional or merely permitted AI use does not count. The activity is named as the AI activity, and students use an AI tool in it themselves (reading an output someone else generated is E4 practice, not E3). Course-wide permission with no such activity is MISSING; a named activity that permits but does not require AI is PARTIAL |
| E4 | That activity includes a **reflection, evaluation or critique** step: students assess the AI output, not just use it | A generic "reflect on your learning" does not count unless it is about the AI's output and the student's decisions about it |
| E5 | The **assessment rubric** grades the **quality of engagement with AI**: the process, not only the final product | A yes/no disclosure box is compliance, not quality. A criterion must describe better and worse engagement (for example: checked the output against independent evidence, recorded what was kept and rejected and why) |

E3, E4 and E5 are one chain: one designated activity, its critique step, its rubric. The toolkit reports the chain as a unit.

## The two competency levels

Neither is better. Choose by course, not by ambition.

- **AI literacy (foundational).** Understand how AI works, recognize its limits, critically evaluate AI-generated content. Conceptual, reflective or analytical activities. The usual starting point for graduate courses.
- **AI fluency (applied).** Hands-on use of AI tools on the discipline's own tasks: choosing tools, writing prompts, interpreting outputs, building AI into professional workflows. Fits capstones, studios and programs where AI use is already part of the work.

## Verdicts

The toolkit uses four words, and only these, for each standard:

- **MEETS**: the evidence is in the supplied material, quoted with its location.
- **PARTIAL**: something is there but falls short of the literal standard; the report says what is short.
- **MISSING**: the material that would hold it was supplied and it is not there.
- **NOT SUPPLIED**: the material that would hold it was not in the export or folder (for example, rubrics live in another tool). Never pass on "it is probably in Canvas".
